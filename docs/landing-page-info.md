# Landing page information

Facts about Nuntia, written for anyone who might use it. No technical terms, no
sales language. Each part is short enough to become a block or an FAQ answer on
the landing page.

---

## What Nuntia is

Nuntia is an email app for Gmail and Outlook that shows each email thread the way
a chat looks: messages one under another, oldest first, newest last.

You can connect more than one address — a work Gmail and a personal Outlook, for
example — and read all of them in one inbox. When the same conversation reaches
two of your addresses, it appears once.

Nuntia sorts your mail into four groups, keeps notes about the people you write
to, and can put a first draft of a reply in the box. You always decide what is
sent.

## Who it is for

- People who read mail on two or more addresses and are tired of checking each
  one separately.
- People whose inbox is a pile of threads, where the real question is "does this
  one need an answer from me?"
- People who want help from AI but want to use their own AI account, see the
  result before it is used, and keep the final say.

## Can I use it?

Nuntia is in private beta.

1. You ask for access with a short form.
2. A person running it reviews the request and approves it.
3. You receive an email with a link. The link works once.
4. You choose a password, connect a mailbox, and you are in.

After that you sign in with your address and password. No price has been set, and
there is no payment step anywhere in the app.

## What you can do

### One inbox for all your addresses

Connect any mix of Gmail and Outlook addresses. They share one inbox, one search
and one set of notes about people. Replies go out from whichever address the
conversation belongs to, and you can choose a different one when it makes sense.

### Threads that read like conversations

Each thread shows its messages in order, with a divider for each day and the
attachments listed with the message they came with. The quoted history, the
greeting and the signature are taken out of the way, so you read what was
actually written. When you reply, your greeting and signature are put back, and
the earlier message is quoted underneath.

### Four groups that tell you what a thread needs

- **Needs action** — someone is waiting on you.
- **Waiting for reply** — you answered, the ball is with them.
- **Information** — nothing is expected from you.
- **Low priority** — it can wait.

You can rename each group and write what it means to you; the inbox shows your
wording. You can also move a thread to a different group yourself.

### Your own sections

You can add sections of your own and describe what belongs there — everything
from one company, everything about invoices, everything from a particular
address. Each section can be as simple as one condition or as broad as a
description you write in your own words.

### Search

One search box covers every message Nuntia has saved: the subject, the topic, the
people on the thread and the text of the mail itself. Results appear as you type.

### Done

When a thread no longer needs anything from you, mark it Done. It leaves the four
groups and sits in its own section, out of the way but still searchable. If
someone writes again, it comes back.

### Notes about people

Every person you exchange mail with can have a small profile: their role and
company, how you know them, what you usually talk about, what they are working
on, and a sentence or two summarising your history. You write it, or let Nuntia
draft it from your correspondence and then edit it.

Nuntia keeps a list of every change it makes to that profile, with what changed
and an undo. It never overwrites something you wrote yourself.

You can also rate a person from 1 to 10 for importance, and that rating counts
when Nuntia decides how much attention a thread deserves.

### Tags — who a person is to you

Tags are short labels you put on people, not on emails. Details are in the next
section.

### Help with replies

Nuntia can suggest a reply. The suggestion appears in the reply box as normal
text: you can change it, ignore it, or send it. Nothing is sent for you.

### Writing a new message

You can start a new email, add attachments, and send it, with the same greeting
and signature rules as a reply. Addresses you have written to before are offered
as you type.

### Read and unread that stays in step

Opening a thread marks it read, and that also happens in Gmail, so your phone and
your browser agree. You can also mark threads read or unread yourself, one at a
time or several at once.

## Tags, explained

Tags answer a different question from the four groups. A group says what a
_thread_ needs from you. A tag says who a _person_ is to you.

A tag is a short word or phrase: `investor`, `client`, `supplier`, `book club`.
One person can have several, and a tag can be used by any number of people.

Each tag can have a description — what the tag is for, written the way you would
explain it to a colleague. For example:

> **investor** — People who have put money into the company. They expect a short
> update every month and should always get a reply within a day.

That description does real work. When Nuntia writes notes about a person, the tag
tells it how that relationship works, not just that the person exists. Tags also
appear on every thread that person is on, so the relationship is visible while
you read.

### What you can do with tags

- **Create a tag** before anyone has it. A tag with no people yet is still a
  definition waiting to be used.
- **Rename or delete** a tag. Deleting a tag removes it from everyone who had it;
  it does not touch their mail.
- **Write a description** yourself, or ask Nuntia to draft one from the mail of
  the people who carry the tag. There is an **Improve** button when you want a
  fresh attempt.
- **Create a tag with no description** and Nuntia drafts one straight away from
  the people already carrying it.
- **See who has a tag** and when each person was added or removed. The tag page
  keeps the last 50 changes, so you can tell why a description says what it says.
- **Get suggestions.** Nuntia can read a person's correspondence and propose tags
  that fit. You can add them one by one, dismiss them, or add all of them at
  once.

### Tags Nuntia adds itself

While Nuntia is updating what it knows about a person, it can add tags it thinks
fit. Those are marked as added by Nuntia, and only those are ever revised or
removed by it. A tag you added is yours: Nuntia will not rename it, remove it or
rewrite it.

### Keeping descriptions current

A description is written at a moment in time, and a tag changes as people join
it. When the membership of a tag has changed since its description was written,
Nuntia treats the description as out of date and brings it up to date the next
time you open the tags page, a few tags at a time. It only does this for
descriptions it wrote. If you wrote one, it stays exactly as you left it until
you change it.

### Where tags show up

Next to the sender in the inbox, and under the people's names at the top of a
thread. That way you can see at a glance that the message is from a client and
not a stranger.

## The AI helpers, explained

Nuntia uses two optional AI helpers. Neither is needed to use the app, and
neither comes included: you connect your own AI account, so the service is yours,
not Nuntia's. You can switch on one, both, or neither.

**One helper makes decisions.** For each thread it asks: how important is this,
how urgent is it, does it need a reply at all, and what group does it belong in
when the simple rules cannot tell? It gives scores, not sentences.

**The other helper writes words.** It names what a thread is about, drafts the
notes about a person, suggests their tags and writes tag descriptions, and
suggests replies.

The two are separate on purpose. If the writing helper is switched off, sorting
still works. If the decision helper is off, topics and drafts still work. With
neither, Nuntia still sorts mail by its built-in rules.

### You choose the AI company

Nuntia works with an account from Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (ChatGPT) or Google
(Gemini). You paste your key into Settings and choose the model. You can change
or remove it at any time, and the key is stored locked.

### When a reply is suggested

Nuntia offers a draft when all of these are true:

- an AI key is set up,
- the thread has somebody a reply can reach,
- you did not send the last message,
- and the thread is important enough.

"Important enough" means 6 out of 10 by default, and you can move that bar. There
is also a setting that asks for a draft on every thread where a reply is
possible, whatever its score.

The draft appears in the reply box. It is not saved anywhere, and it is never
sent automatically. Nuntia also removes a greeting or sign-off the AI
wrote, because your own greeting and signature are added when you send.

### What the AI is given

For a draft, Nuntia sends the conversation, the subject, the topic, your goal for
the thread, your notes about the person, and anything you typed as an instruction
for that one draft. For notes and tags, it sends the relevant correspondence and
the notes you already have.

Its answers are proposals. Apart from tags and contact notes, which you can let it
keep up to date, nothing changes in your mail until you act on it.

### What the AI does not do

- It does not send anything.
- It does not decide for you whether a thread is finished.
- It does not replace the built-in rules; those run first and win where they
  apply.

### A record of AI use

The person running Nuntia can see a log of every AI request: which service, which
model, whether it worked and how long it took. The log records no email text and
no message content.

## Where your mail lives

- Nuntia keeps its own copy of your mail in one file on the machine where it
  runs. That is what makes search and reading fast.
- Your Google or Microsoft sign-in and your AI key are stored locked, readable
  only with a key the person running Nuntia holds.
- Nuntia asks for permission to read your mail, send mail, and mark it read. It
  cannot delete anything, in Nuntia or in your mailbox.
- Mail is cleaned before it is shown: scripts and other active content are
  removed, and the message is displayed in a way that keeps it from affecting the
  rest of the page.
- A draft is not kept after you leave the reply box.
- Photos next to names are optional. By default you see initials, and nothing
  about the person is sent anywhere to fetch a picture.
- The person running Nuntia can make backups of everything it stores.
- There is no third-party tracking in the app. The usage stats the beta relies on
  are kept in Nuntia's own file.

Three things leave the machine, and only when they should: mail is read from and
sent to Google or Microsoft; text goes to your AI service when a helper is
switched on; and a photo is requested from a picture service called Gravatar if
you turn that on yourself. Nothing else is sent anywhere.

## What you need

- A Gmail or Outlook address — one of them is enough.
- Either an invitation from whoever runs the Nuntia you are joining, or a
  computer of your own to run it on.
- To run your own, you register a small app with Google or Microsoft once, so they
  know Nuntia is allowed to connect. The setup guides walk through it, and it
  takes a few minutes per provider.
- The first connection brings in the last 30 days of mail. Older mail is not
  fetched.
- Nuntia checks for new mail on a timer. With extra setup, Gmail can tell it the
  moment something arrives instead.

## Not there yet

- Only Gmail and Outlook. No other mail providers.
- No spam handling, spam rules or deleted-mail view.
- No phone app and no desktop app. It runs in the browser.
- No shared workspace for a team. Everyone has their own.
- No calendar, and no address book imported from Google or Microsoft. The people
  you see are the people you have exchanged mail with.
- No scheduled sending and no automatic replies.
- AI needs your own key from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. There is no included
  model.
- Tags show on threads but do not yet change the suggested reply; only the topic
  and goal do.
- Mail does not arrive instantly unless Gmail's extra setup is done.

## Words the app uses

- **Thread** — one email conversation, with all its messages.
- **Group** — one of the four built-in buckets a thread sits in.
- **Section** — a bucket you create yourself, with rules or a description.
- **Topic** — a few words naming what a thread is about.
- **Goal** — what you want out of a thread, in your own words.
- **Importance** — a score from 1 to 10.
- **Done** — a thread you have dealt with.
- **Contact** — a person you write to.
- **Tag** — a label on a person, with an optional description.
- **Draft** — a suggested reply, editable, never sent by itself.

## Claims to avoid

- "All your email in one place" — only Gmail and Outlook.
- "Instant" or "real-time" — new mail is checked on a timer unless Gmail's extra
  setup is done.
- "Your mail never leaves your server" — it goes to Google or Microsoft, and to
  your AI provider when a helper is on.
- "Fully encrypted" — sign-ins and AI keys are locked at rest; that is not the
  same as end-to-end encryption.
- "Unlimited" anything — no plan or limit promise exists.
- "Free forever" — pricing is undecided.
- "Built for teams" — there is no shared workspace.
- "AI that replies for you" — it drafts; you send.
- "Reads your mail so you don't have to" — sorting is rules first, and the AI
  helpers are opt-in.
